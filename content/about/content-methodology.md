## How we research a guide

1. **Start with the official requirement.** We read the current requirements published by the agency that sets them — for U.S. passports, the Department of State's passport photo page, application form instructions, and the Foreign Affairs Manual where relevant.
2. **Record the exact rule.** We note measurements and limits exactly as published (for example, a 2 × 2 inch photo with a 1 to 1⅜ inch head) before rewriting anything in plain English.
3. **Convert and explain.** Where helpful, we convert units (inches to millimeters and pixels) and show our working, such as using 300 DPI for print-to-pixel conversions.
4. **Explain the practical side.** We add practical tips, such as how to light a photo at home. These are clearly our own advice, not official rules.
5. **Link the sources.** Every guide lists the official pages it was checked against, so you can verify anything yourself.

## How we keep guides current

Official requirements change occasionally — for example, the U.S. stopped accepting eyeglasses in passport photos in 2016. We recheck each guide against its sources regularly and when we hear about a change. The "last updated" date on each guide shows the most recent meaningful update.

## How we build and test tools

Our tools use the same measurements as our guides. The passport photo maker, for example, sets the head height between 50% and 69% of a 2 × 2 inch photo, matching the official 1 to 1⅜ inch range. We test tools in current versions of major browsers on desktop and mobile. All processing happens on your device; we don't collect photos to test or train anything.

## Limits you should know about

- **We are not the decision-maker.** The agency reviewing your application decides whether a photo is acceptable. A photo that meets every published rule can still be rejected — for example, if it's too dark or doesn't look like you.
- **Rules can change before we update.** Always confirm the current requirements on the official source before you apply, especially if your application is urgent.
- **Your situation may be different.** Special cases — such as medical devices, religious attire, or photos of infants — have extra rules. We summarize them, but the agency's instructions take priority.
- **Our tools don't check compliance.** They crop, resize, and compress. They don't assess lighting, background, expression, or image quality.

## Questions or corrections

If you think a guide is wrong, out of date, or unclear, please [contact us](/contact) and include a link to the official source if you have one.
