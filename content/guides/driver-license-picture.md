Your driver license picture is taken at the DMV, on the day you apply or renew. In most states, you cannot upload your own photo, so a little preparation goes a long way. This guide explains the DMV photo requirements, what to wear, whether you can smile or keep your glasses on, and what to do if you want a retake.

Rules change by state, so check your DMV's official page before you go. **What this guide is based on:** the only state agency we cite is Pennsylvania's PennDOT, so the Pennsylvania details are sourced and the rest is general guidance that applies in most states. ## How the DMV Takes Your Driver License Picture

The DMV photo is part of your application, not a separate errand. You file your paperwork, the clerk verifies your documents, and then you step up to the camera.

### Photo Steps for a New License or DMV Photo ID

1. **Gather your documents.** Bring proof of identity, residency and any other items your state requires.
2. **Complete the application.** A clerk reviews your paperwork.
3. **Step up to the camera.** Staff position you, check your expression and take the photo.
4. **Review the image.** Many offices show you the result on a screen, which is your chance to ask for a retake.
5. **Leave with your card or temporary paper license.** Your permanent card usually arrives by mail.

The same steps apply when you get a DMV photo ID card. A photo ID card follows the same photo rules as a driver's license.

### How the Photo Works in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania adds one extra step for renewals. PennDOT mails you a **camera card**, which usually arrives within seven to ten business days after your renewal is processed. You then bring the camera card and an accepted form of ID to a Photo License Center, where staff take your photo and issue your new license. PennDOT also uses facial recognition when it takes renewal photos.

### When the DMV Takes a New Photo

You usually sit for a new photo when you:

- Apply for your first license or learner's permit
- Renew an expired or soon-to-expire license
- Upgrade to a REAL ID-compliant card
- Replace a lost or damaged card
- Change your name or appearance significantly

## DMV Photo Requirements You Must Follow

Every state follows federal standards for secure IDs, then adds its own rules. These requirements appear in nearly every state.

### Facial Expression and Eyes

- Face the camera straight on, with your head level.
- Keep your eyes open and visible.
- Use a neutral expression or a slight, closed-mouth smile.

### Face Visibility and Hair

- Show your full face, from your forehead to your chin.
- Move hair away from your eyes and eyebrows.
- Remove anything that casts shadows on your face.

### Hats and Head Coverings

DMVs generally do not allow hats, caps or headbands. Religious and medical head coverings are the usual exceptions, and your face must still be fully visible. Some states ask for a signed statement. Check your state's rules before your visit.

### Makeup, Jewelry and Piercings

- Wear light makeup that matches how you normally look. Heavy contouring can make staff question whether the photo matches your face.
- Skip large, reflective earrings and necklaces that sit near your jaw.
- Keep everyday piercings in. Remove anything that hides part of your face.

### Colored Contact Lenses

Your license lists your eye color. Remove colored or cosmetic lenses that change your eye color so the photo matches your card.

## How DMV Photo Rules Vary by State

Federal law sets the baseline for secure IDs, and each state fills in the details. We have not compiled a state-by-state table, because we only cite one state's official pages. The two questions that vary most are smiles and glasses, so check your own state's DMV page for both.

### Smiling in Your DMV Photo

Most DMVs prefer a neutral expression or a slight, closed-mouth smile because facial recognition software compares the shape of your mouth and cheeks. Some states are more relaxed than others, so follow your state's instructions and the clerk at the camera.

**Safest choice:** Relax your face and keep your lips together, with a hint of a smile in your eyes.

### Glasses in Your DMV Photo

Glare on lenses can interfere with facial recognition, so many states ask you to remove your glasses. Most also offer a medical exception when you cannot remove them. You usually need a signed statement from a doctor, and an ordinary prescription does not qualify on its own.

Online guides often contradict each other on this point because state rules differ. Always confirm with your state's DMV, and follow the clerk's instructions at the camera.

#### Can You Wear Glasses in a DMV Photo? Quick Rules

- **Sunglasses and tinted lenses:** Avoid them in every state.
- **Clear prescription glasses:** Allowed in some states, banned in others.
- **Glare or thick frames:** Staff will ask you to remove glasses that block your eyes or reflect light.
- **Medical need:** Bring documentation if you cannot remove them.

#### What If You Need Glasses to Drive?

Your license may list a corrective lens restriction. That restriction depends on your vision test, not on whether you wear glasses in the photo. Take the vision test, then follow the photo rules at the camera.

## What to Wear for a DMV Photo

The camera captures your head and the top of your shoulders, so your neckline and color matter more than your outfit.

### Best Color to Wear for a DMV Photo

Choose a solid color that contrasts with the backdrop and flatters your skin tone.

- **Good picks:** Navy, forest green, burgundy, teal and charcoal.
- **Use caution with:** Light pastels, which can wash you out.
- **Avoid white** when the DMV uses a white or light background, because your shirt will blend into it.

### What to Wear to a DMV Photo: Do and Don't

| Do wear                               | Don't wear                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| A solid-color top                     | Busy patterns or stripes                        |
| A neckline that shows your collarbone | Turtlenecks or high collars that hide your neck |
| Clothes you wear every day            | Large logos, slogans or uniforms                |
| Light, natural makeup                 | Hats, sunglasses or heavy accessories           |

Wear something comfortable. You will feel more relaxed, and a relaxed face photographs better.

## How to Take a Good DMV Photo

You usually get one or two attempts, so use these steps to get it right the first time.

1. **Practice your expression at home.** Look into your phone camera and rehearse a relaxed, closed-mouth look.
2. **Get a good night's sleep.** Rest reduces puffy eyes and dark circles.
3. **Style your hair away from your face.** Tuck hair behind your ears if needed.
4. **Pick a solid, contrasting top.** Follow the color tips above.
5. **Bring any medical or religious documentation.** Staff can then approve your glasses or head covering without delay.
6. **Stand tall and look at the lens.** Keep your chin level and your shoulders relaxed.
7. **Breathe out right before the shot.** A quick exhale loosens your jaw.
8. **Review the image if the DMV shows it.** Ask politely for a retake if your eyes were closed or your expression looked off.

## Can You Retake Your DMV Photo?

Yes, sometimes. DMV policies on a DMV photo retake vary by state and by office, and the clerk makes the final call.

### Asking for a Retake at the Counter

Many offices allow one more attempt if you ask right away, especially if you blinked or the image looks distorted. Ask politely and say what went wrong. Staff are more likely to agree when the problem is technical, such as closed eyes or poor framing, than when you simply dislike how you look.

### Changing a Photo After You Leave

Once the DMV issues your card, you generally cannot swap the photo for free. You usually need to apply for a replacement card and pay the fee, and your office takes a new photo as part of that process. A bad driver license picture is something you may carry for years, so take the review step seriously before you walk away.

## How to Find Your DMV Photo Office and Hours

Your state's official DMV location finder is the best place to check hours and services, because they change. Third-party listings are often out of date.

1. Visit your state DMV or licensing agency website.
2. Open the location or office finder.
3. Check whether the office takes photos, since some locations only handle exams or registrations.
4. Confirm the hours, and book an appointment if your state offers them.

Hours differ by state and by branch. Pennsylvania notes that demand for photo services peaks at the start and end of each month, so wait times run shorter on other days; plan a mid-month visit if your schedule allows. If you are renewing in Pennsylvania, bring your camera card to the Photo License Center, because renewal photo services require it.

## Special Situations and Edge Cases

### Teen Drivers and First-Time Applicants

First-time applicants take the photo at the end of their application visit. Teens follow the same photo rules as adults. Parents or guardians may need to attend, depending on the state.

### Renewing Online or by Mail

Some states let you renew online or by mail and reuse your photo on file. Others require a new photo at least every few renewals. Pennsylvania mails a camera card after an online or mail renewal, so you still visit a Photo License Center for your new photo and card.

### REAL ID Photos

A REAL ID card uses the same photo process as a standard license. The difference is the paperwork: you must show extra proof of identity and residency. Federal enforcement for domestic flights and federal buildings began May 7, 2025, so check your state's REAL ID checklist before you book a visit.

### Religious or Medical Head Coverings

You can wear a head covering for religious or medical reasons in most states, as long as your face stays visible from forehead to chin. Ask your DMV whether you need a signed statement.

### Injuries, Swelling and Recent Surgery

Facial swelling, bruising or a bandage can make your photo hard to match to your future appearance. If you can, reschedule until the swelling goes down. If you cannot wait, ask the clerk what your office allows.

### Commercial Driver's Licenses

A CDL uses the same photo rules as a standard license, but it adds testing and documentation requirements. Check those requirements before your visit so the photo is the last step, not a surprise.

### Name Changes and Appearance Changes

A new hair color, a beard change or a name change does not break any rule, but your photo should look like you on the day you visit. Bring your name-change documents with you.

## Can You Use Your Own Photo for a Driver's License?

In most states, you cannot. The DMV takes the photo on site, and a pharmacy photo, selfie or print-shop picture does not count for a standard in-person license. A few states allow a photo upload during certain online renewals. Check your DMV's website to see whether yours does.

### DMV Photo vs. Passport Photo

Do not confuse your DMV photo with a passport photo. A U.S. passport requires a separate 2 × 2 inch photo that you provide, and the head size and background rules differ. ## Common DMV Photo Mistakes

- **Wearing white against a white backdrop.** Your shirt disappears into the background.
- **Smiling too wide.** A toothy grin can trigger a retake.
- **Forgetting documentation.** Staff cannot approve a medical or religious exception without it.
- **Ignoring the review screen.** You lose your best chance to ask for a retake.
- **Wearing a high collar.** It hides your neck line and affects the framing.
- **Arriving rushed.** Stress shows on your face, so give yourself extra time.
- **Skipping the official page.** State rules differ, so a generic guide can be wrong for your state.

## Your DMV Photo Checklist

Before you leave home, confirm that you have:

- [ ] A solid-color top with a visible neckline
- [ ] Hair away from your face
- [ ] Light, natural makeup
- [ ] No hats, sunglasses or colored contacts
- [ ] Documents for any glasses or head-covering exception
- [ ] Your camera card, if your state uses one for renewals
- [ ] Your appointment confirmation, if you booked one

A great driver license picture comes down to three things: follow your state's rules, wear a contrasting solid color, and keep your expression relaxed and natural. Check your DMV's official page, practice your look at home, and ask for a retake at the counter if the first shot misses.
