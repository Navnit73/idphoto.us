A passport photo is one of the most common reasons a U.S. application gets delayed. The rules are specific, but none of them are complicated once you see them together. This guide covers every requirement the U.S. Department of State publishes for passport photos, grouped by what you need to get right: size, appearance, background and lighting, and printing. Each section starts with the short answer, then explains the detail.

If you only remember three things: the photo must be **2 × 2 inches**, your head must be **1 to 1⅜ inches** from chin to top of hair, and the background must be **plain white or off-white**.

## The U.S. passport photo specs at a glance

| Requirement | What the rule says |
| --- | --- |
| Photo size | 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) |
| Head height | 1 to 1⅜ inches (25–35 mm), chin to top of hair |
| Background | Plain white or off-white, no shadows or patterns |
| Color | Color photo, in focus |
| Age of photo | Taken within the last six months |
| Glasses | Not allowed (medical exception needs a signed statement) |
| Expression | Neutral or natural smile, both eyes open, facing the camera |
| Editing | No filters, retouching, or AI changes. Cropping and resizing are fine |
| Print | Matte or glossy photo-quality paper, undamaged |

The rules come from the Department of State's [passport photo page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/passports/how-apply/photos.html) and the Foreign Affairs Manual. ## What size is a U.S. passport photo?

A U.S. passport photo is **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)**, and your head must measure 1 to 1⅜ inches (25–35 mm) from the bottom of your chin to the top of your hair. The photo is square, with your head centered and your full face shown from the front.

Head size is where most home photos go wrong. A photo taken too close makes the head too large; one taken from too far away makes it too small. If your original is not square, you need to crop it, and the crop has to keep the head inside the 1 to 1⅜ inch range once the photo is 2 × 2 inches. ### How do I measure head height?

Measure from the bottom of your chin to the top of your hair, not the top of your skull. On a 2-inch square, that means your head should fill roughly half to two-thirds of the height. Hair volume counts, so a tall hairstyle can push you over the limit even when your face looks small. If you are unsure, run the photo through the [passport photo checker](/checker/test-passport-photo) before you submit.

## What should I do with my face and pose?

Face the camera directly, with your full face in view, a neutral expression or a natural smile, and both eyes open. Don't tilt your head, and keep your shoulders square to the camera.

- Look straight into the lens rather than past it.
- Keep your mouth relaxed. A big, wide smile can change your face enough to cause a rejection.
- Keep your hair off your face so that both eyes and eyebrows are visible.

### Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?

No. Glasses are **not allowed** in U.S. passport photos. The only exception is when you can't remove them for medical reasons, such as recovery from eye surgery. In that case you must include a signed statement from your doctor with your application.

### Can I wear a hat or head covering?

Only for religious or medical reasons, and only with a signed statement. Otherwise, remove hats and head coverings.

- **Religious:** a statement that verifies the head covering is part of recognized, traditional religious attire worn continuously in public.
- **Medical:** a signed statement from a doctor that the head covering is used daily for medical purposes.

Even with a statement, your full face must be visible and the covering must not cast shadows on your face.

### What can I wear in a passport photo?

Wear everyday clothing. Uniforms, clothing that looks like a uniform, and camouflage are not acceptable. Remove headphones and wireless hands-free devices. Jewelry and facial piercings are fine as long as they don't hide your face, and permanent tattoos are acceptable.

A practical tip, not an official rule: because the background is white or off-white, a solid mid-tone or dark top gives clearer contrast than a white shirt.

## What background and lighting do I need?

Use a **plain white or off-white background** with no patterns, objects, or other people in the frame. Light your face evenly so there are no shadows on your face or behind you.

A window with indirect daylight in front of you works well. Avoid overhead lights that shadow your eyes, and avoid a flash that creates a harsh shadow on the wall behind you. The photo must be in **color** and in focus.

### Can I use filters, retouching, or AI?

No. Don't use filters or apps that smooth your skin, change your features, or replace the background. The Department of State doesn't accept photos changed with software, filters, or artificial intelligence. Cropping and resizing to the right dimensions is fine; changing how you look is not.

### How recent does the photo need to be?

Taken within the last six months, and it must show how you look now. If your appearance has changed noticeably since then, for example a major haircut, facial hair, or weight change, take a new photo.

## How should I print my passport photo?

Print on **matte or glossy photo-quality paper**, at actual size with no scaling, so the 2 × 2 inch dimensions stay exact. The photo must not be damaged: no creases, holes, smudges, or marks. Don't staple it through your face.

Most people print at a pharmacy or photo lab. Our [print passport photos page](/tool/print-passport-photos) explains the 4 × 6 inch sheet layout, which fits more than one 2 × 2 photo on a single print.

## Can I upload a digital photo instead?

Yes, for online renewal. If you renew online, you upload a digital photo instead of mailing a print. The appearance rules above still apply. The Department of State publishes separate technical instructions for the upload, including file format and size limits; check the [official upload page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/passports/how-apply/online-renewal-photo.html) before you start, because those details can change.

## What are the rules for babies and children?

Children need their own photo, and the same rules apply. For babies, no other person or object (such as a hand or a toy) may appear in the photo. A simple approach is to lay the baby on a plain white sheet and take the photo from above. It's acceptable if a newborn's eyes aren't fully open.

## Can I take my passport photo at home?

Yes. Many people take their own passport photo at home. Stand a few feet from a plain white or off-white wall in even light, ask someone to take the picture at eye level, then crop to 2 × 2 inches with the correct head size.

Home photos fail for a short list of reasons. Use this to check yours:

- A shadow on the wall behind you or across your face.
- A head that is too large or too small after cropping.
- Glasses, glare, or a tilted head.
- A wall that is gray, cream, or patterned rather than white or off-white.
- Heavy filters or "beauty" modes that smooth skin or change features.

## What are the most common reasons a passport photo is rejected?

The most common problems are the ones above: wrong head size, shadows, a background that isn't white or off-white, glasses, and edited or filtered images. Each one is easy to avoid if you check before you submit. We don't have official rejection statistics, and the agency makes the final decision on every photo, so treat any tool, including ours, as a way to catch obvious problems rather than a guarantee.

If a photo is rejected, you generally have to submit a new one, which can delay your application. That is the reason to spend a few minutes on the checklist below.

## Can I reuse an old passport photo?

Only if it was taken within the last six months and still looks like you. The age limit applies to every application, whether you are applying for the first time, renewing, or replacing a lost passport. A photo from an earlier passport is almost always too old, and an old print may also be scratched or faded. Take a fresh photo instead.

## How do I fix a photo with shadows or a bad background?

Retake it. Software can crop and resize a photo, but the Department of State does not accept photos where the background was replaced or your appearance was altered, so a shadow on the wall is best fixed with better lighting, not editing.

Try this setup, which is practical advice rather than an official rule:

1. Stand about two feet in front of a plain white or off-white wall.
2. Face a window so daylight lights your face evenly.
3. Have someone hold the camera at eye level, about four feet away.
4. Take several photos and pick the sharpest one.
5. Crop it to 2 × 2 inches with your head between 1 and 1⅜ inches.

## Is a passport photo the same as a visa photo?

Not always. The U.S. passport photo is 2 × 2 inches, and the U.S. visa photo for the DS-160 online form uses a square digital file with its own technical limits.
