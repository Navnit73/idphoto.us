The official size of a U.S. passport photo is **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)**. That part is easy. What trips people up is the second measurement: the size of your head inside the photo, which must be **between 1 inch and 1⅜ inches (25–35 mm)** from the bottom of your chin to the top of your hair.

This guide gives you both measurements in every unit you're likely to need, shows how to check a photo, and explains how to fix the most common sizing problems.

## The two measurements that matter

![Diagram of a 2 by 2 inch passport photo showing the head measured from chin to top of hair at 1 to 1⅜ inches](/images/guides/passport-photo-dimensions.svg "The photo is 2 × 2 in. The head, chin to top of hair, is 1–1⅜ in.")

### Photo size

The finished photo is a square: **2 inches wide by 2 inches tall**. Printed photos that are even slightly off — 2 × 2.5 inches, for example, or a wallet print — won't be accepted.

### Head size

Measure from the **bottom of the chin** to the **top of the hair** (not the top of the forehead). The result must be between **1 inch and 1⅜ inches**. As a share of the photo's height, that's **50% to about 69%**. The head should also be centered horizontally.

## Sizes in inches, millimeters, and pixels

Digital photos don't have a physical size until they're printed. The standard for photo printing is 300 dots per inch (DPI), so a 2-inch side becomes 600 pixels.

| Measurement | Inches | Millimeters | Pixels at 600 × 600 | Pixels at 1200 × 1200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photo width and height | 2 | 51 | 600 | 1200 |
| Minimum head height | 1 | 25 | 300 | 600 |
| Maximum head height | 1⅜ | 35 | 412 | 825 |

For U.S. visa applications, the Department of State asks for a **square JPEG between 600 × 600 and 1200 × 1200 pixels, no larger than 240 KB**, with the head taking up 50% to 69% of the image height. If your file is too large, re-save it as a JPEG at a lower quality setting, keeping it at least 600 × 600 pixels.

## How to check your photo's size

### For a printed photo

1. Measure the photo with a ruler. Each side should be exactly 2 inches.
2. Measure from the bottom of the chin to the top of the hair. It should be between 1 and 1⅜ inches.
3. Check that the face is centered left to right.

### For a digital photo

1. Check the pixel dimensions in your phone's photo info or your computer's file properties. Width and height must be equal.
2. Open the image and estimate the head height as a fraction of the image height. Between half and roughly two-thirds is right.
3. Check the file size against the limit of the form you're using.

## Common sizing mistakes and how to fix them

### The head is too small

This usually happens when the photo was taken from too far away, or the crop includes too much of the shoulders and background. Zoom in when cropping so the head fills more of the frame. If the original photo is low resolution, zooming in may make it blurry — retake it closer instead.

### The head is too large

The photo was taken too close, or cropped too tightly. Retake it with the camera a few feet away. Close-up photos taken at arm's length can also distort facial proportions, which is another reason to have someone else take the photo.

### The photo isn't square

Phone photos are rectangular, so they always need cropping. Crop to a square before printing, and make sure your printer doesn't stretch the image to fill a different paper size.

### The print came out the wrong size

Printing software often scales images to fit the page. Choose "actual size" or "100%" and turn off borderless or fit-to-page options. A 4 × 6 inch print is a convenient format: it fits exactly six 2 × 2 inch photos.

## Other documents that use the same size

Many U.S. applications ask for passport-style photos, including U.S. visa applications and a number of USCIS immigration forms. Always check the specific form's instructions, because some have their own requirements. Photos for other countries' passports often use different sizes — 35 × 45 mm is common in Europe — so don't assume a U.S. passport photo will work abroad.
