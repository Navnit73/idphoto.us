Applying for a U.S. visa means uploading a digital photo with your DS-160 (or DS-260 for immigrant visas), and the upload rejects files that break its technical rules. Upload a photo above and the editor crops it to a square, positions your head at the size the Department of State asks for, checks the background and saves a 600 × 600 pixel JPEG ready for the form.

## Make a DS-160 Ready Photo Online

1. **Keep "United States" selected.** It produces a square 600 × 600 pixel photo, the minimum size the DS-160 accepts.
2. **Upload a photo.** Use a recent, front-facing photo taken in even light against a plain white or off-white wall, without glasses. Your browser re-saves it before uploading, which also removes hidden metadata such as GPS location.
3. **Wait a moment.** Processing can take up to a minute. Keep the page open until your result appears.
4. **Check the result.** The result page shows the pixel size, file size, head height, eye line and background check, so you can compare them with the rules below before you upload to the DS-160.

The photo is uploaded over an encrypted connection, because face detection and background checks run on a server. Your result page expires after 7 days.

## Digital Visa Photo File Specifications

The Department of State publishes [digital image requirements](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/photos/digital-image-requirements.html) for visa photos. The DS-160 checks your file against them when you upload it.

| Requirement  | U.S. visa digital photo                         |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Dimensions   | 600 × 600 to 1200 × 1200 pixels                 |
| Aspect ratio | Square (width equals height)                    |
| Format       | JPEG (.jpg)                                     |
| File size    | 240 kB or less                                  |
| Head height  | 50–69% of the image height, chin to top of head |
| Background   | Plain white or off-white                        |
| Age of photo | Taken in the last 6 months                      |

### Pixel Dimensions and Square Aspect Ratio

The photo must be square, at least 600 × 600 pixels and no more than 1200 × 1200 pixels. A photo that is 600 × 601 pixels, or a portrait-shaped phone picture, will be rejected. The editor outputs exactly 600 × 600 pixels, so the dimensions and aspect ratio are always right.

Inside that square, your head must take up 50–69% of the image height, measured from the bottom of your chin to the top of your head. In a 600 × 600 photo, that's 300 to 414 pixels. The editor crops to this range and reports the head height it measured.

### JPEG Format and Maximum File Size

The file must be a JPEG no larger than **240 kB**. Photos straight from a phone are usually several megabytes, and iPhones often save HEIC rather than JPEG, so they fail without conversion. The editor saves a JPEG, and the result page shows its file size. If it's ever above 240 kB, re-save it in a photo editor at a slightly lower JPEG quality without changing its pixel size.

## Crop a Photo for the US Visa Without Losing Quality

Cropping throws pixels away, so a sharp result starts with enough detail in the original:

- **Use the full-resolution original**, not a screenshot or a photo saved from a messaging app, which are often compressed.
- **Have someone else take the photo** from about 4 feet away. Selfies taken at arm's length distort the face and often crop the shoulders.
- **Don't zoom in digitally** when you take the picture. Step closer instead.
- **Crop and resize once.** Every re-save of a JPEG loses a little quality, so avoid editing the photo in several apps before uploading it.

The editor works from your original and scales it down to 600 × 600 pixels in a single step, which keeps the result sharp as long as your face is in focus.

## Check Your Visa Photo Before Uploading

Before you upload to the DS-160, compare your result with this checklist:

- Square, 600 × 600 to 1200 × 1200 pixels, JPEG, 240 kB or less.
- Head height between 50% and 69% of the photo, with your face centered.
- Plain white or off-white background with no shadows, patterns or objects.
- Full face looking straight at the camera, neutral expression, both eyes open.
- No glasses, and no hat or head covering unless worn daily for religious reasons.
- Taken in the last six months, in color, and not edited to change how you look.

The editor checks the size, head height, eye line and background for you. It can't judge your expression, lighting or clothing, and it isn't an official check: the consular officer decides whether your photo is accepted.

### Fixing the 'Photo Failed to Upload' Error

If the DS-160 rejects your photo, the cause is usually one of these:

- **Wrong file.** The photo isn't square, is outside 600–1200 pixels, isn't a JPEG or is larger than 240 kB. Re-make it with the editor and check the file size on the result page.
- **Head too large or too small.** Retake the photo from a bit farther away or closer, then crop it again.
- **Background or lighting.** Shadows, a colored wall or uneven light can fail the photo quality check. Retake the photo facing a window against a plain white wall.

If the photo still won't upload, you can usually continue with the application. According to the Department of State's [photo FAQ](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/photos/frequently-asked-questions.html), you then bring one printed photo that meets the requirements to your interview, with your confirmation page. Check with your embassy or consulate for its exact instructions.

## Printed Visa Photos for the Consulate Interview

Some applicants need a printed photo: immigrant visa applicants bring two identical photos to the interview, and anyone whose DS-160 photo upload failed brings one. Some embassies also ask for a photo even when the upload worked, so read your interview instructions.

Printed visa photos must be **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)**, in color, on matte or glossy photo-quality paper. The 600 × 600 pixel file from this editor prints at exactly 2 × 2 inches at 300 DPI; print at actual size with no scaling.
