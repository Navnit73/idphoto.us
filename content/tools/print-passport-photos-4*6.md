Your passport photo is ready, and now you need it on paper. This page shows three ways to print it: order prints delivered, send a 4 × 6 file to a store for pickup, or print it on your own printer. It also gives exact layouts for fitting two, four or six 2 × 2 inch photos on one 4 × 6 sheet. Don't have a correctly sized photo yet? Make one with the tool above first.

## Three Ways to Get Printed Passport Photos

All three start with the same thing: a digital photo at the right size. For a U.S. passport, visa or green card, that's a 600 × 600 pixel photo, which prints at 2 × 2 inches at 300 DPI. idphoto.us doesn't print or mail photos; you choose where to print.

### Order Professional Prints Delivered to Your Door

Most online photo print services sell standard 4 × 6 inch prints and mail them to you. Upload a 4 × 6 layout (see the [template below](#free-4x6-passport-photo-template)) instead of the single photo, so you get several passport photos on one print. Allow for shipping time; if your appointment is soon, use store pickup instead.

### Send a 4x6 File to Walgreens, CVS or Walmart

Pharmacies and big-box stores such as Walgreens, CVS and Walmart sell 4 × 6 photo prints that you can order online and collect in store, often the same day. Upload your 1800 × 1200 pixel layout as a regular 4 × 6 print, not as a passport photo order, then cut the photos out at home.

Before you order, check the print preview. If the service offers a "crop to fit" or "fill" option, make sure your layout isn't zoomed or trimmed. Ordering one print at actual size and measuring it is cheaper than reprinting a batch.

### Print on Your Own Inkjet Printer

A color inkjet printer and a pack of 4 × 6 inch photo paper are enough. Many inkjets can't print right to the edge of the paper, so use the two-photo layout, which leaves a margin on every side. You can also print on letter-size photo paper: place the 600 × 600 photos with space between them on an 8.5 × 11 inch page (2550 × 3300 pixels at 300 DPI) and print at actual size.

## Free 4x6 Passport Photo Template

You don't need special software to make a 4 × 6 sheet. In any photo editor that lets you paste one image onto another:

1. Create a new image **1800 pixels wide and 1200 pixels tall** with a white background. That's 6 × 4 inches at 300 DPI.
2. Paste your 600 × 600 pixel passport photo onto it, and place the copies at the positions in the table below. Don't resize the photo.
3. Save as a high-quality JPEG and print it as a 4 × 6 photo.

### Layout for Two, Four or Six Photos per Sheet

Positions are the top-left corner of each 600 × 600 photo, measured in pixels from the top-left corner of the 1800 × 1200 canvas.

| Photos | Positions (x, y) | Margins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two | (200, 300) and (1000, 300) | 200 px (⅔ in) between and beside, 300 px (1 in) above and below |
| Four | (200, 0), (1000, 0), (200, 600), (1000, 600) | 200 px between the columns; the rows touch the top and bottom edges |
| Six | (0, 0), (600, 0), (1200, 0), (0, 600), (600, 600), (1200, 600) | None: the photos fill the whole sheet |

Two 2 × 2 inch rows take up the full 4 inches of a 4 × 6 print, so layouts with four or six photos run to the edge of the paper. Home printers and some print services can trim or skip a thin strip at the edges, which would make the outer photos slightly too small. Most U.S. applications ask for one or two photos, so the two-photo layout is the safest choice; print a second sheet if you need more.

## Best Photo Paper and Printer Settings

### Glossy vs. Matte Photo Paper for Passports

The Department of State accepts U.S. passport and visa photos printed on **matte or glossy photo-quality paper**. Plain paper, cardstock and laser prints don't qualify. Whichever finish you choose, the photo must not be creased, smudged or marked.

If you're printing photos for a green card or another USCIS form, check the form's instructions. The [Form I-485 instructions](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-485instr.pdf), for example, ask for photos on thin glossy paper. Glossy paper is therefore the safer choice if you'll use the same prints for both a passport and an immigration form.

### Turning Off 'Fit to Page' Scaling

The most common printing mistake is letting the printer resize the photo. In the print dialog:

- Set the paper size to **4 × 6 in** (or letter, if that's what you loaded) and the paper type to **photo paper**, glossy or matte to match.
- Turn off **Fit to page**, **Scale to fit** or **Fill frame**, and choose **Actual size** or **100%** scale.
- Choose the highest print quality and color printing.
- Turn off borderless printing for the two-photo layout. Borderless mode slightly enlarges the image so it reaches the edges.

Print one sheet and measure a photo with a ruler before printing more. Each photo must be exactly 2 inches (51 mm) on each side.

## How to Cut Passport Photos Accurately

- **Let the ink dry** for a few minutes before you handle the print, so it doesn't smudge.
- **Use a paper trimmer**, or a metal ruler and a craft knife on a cutting mat. Freehand scissors make wavy edges.
- **Cut exactly along the photo's edge**, not inside it. Trimming even a sixteenth of an inch makes the photo smaller than 2 × 2 inches.
- **Measure after cutting.** Each photo should be a true 2-inch square.
- **Keep the photos flat and clean.** Don't fold them or touch the face area, and don't staple them through your face when you attach them to a form.
