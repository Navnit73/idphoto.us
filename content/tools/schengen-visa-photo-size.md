## How to make a Schengen visa photo online

1. **Keep "Schengen visa" selected.** The tool produces a 35 × 45 mm photo as a 630 × 810 pixel file.
2. **Upload a photo.** Use a recent, front-facing photo taken in even light against a plain white or light gray wall. Your browser re-saves it before uploading, which also removes hidden metadata such as GPS location.
3. **Wait a moment.** Processing can take up to a minute. Keep the page open until your result appears.
4. **Check the result.** The result page shows the head size, eye line and background check, so you can compare them with the rules before you print.
5. **Print at actual size.** Print with no scaling so the photo measures exactly 35 × 45 mm. The [print passport photos page](/tool/print-passport-photos) puts several copies on one 4 × 6 inch sheet.

The photo is uploaded over an encrypted connection, because face detection and background checks run on a server. Your result page expires after 7 days.

## Why a U.S. passport photo doesn't work

A U.S. passport photo is a 2 × 2 inch square. A Schengen photo is a 35 × 45 mm rectangle with a larger face. Trimming a 2 × 2 photo rarely works because the head in a U.S. photo can be smaller than the 32 mm minimum, and trimming doesn't make a face bigger. Start from your original picture instead. If you also need U.S. photos, use the [2x2 photo maker](/tool/2x2-photo-maker).

## Schengen visa photos at VFS Global and BLS appointments

In the U.S., most Schengen visa applications are handed in at an application center run by VFS Global or BLS International for the consulate. Staff check your documents, including the photo, when you hand them in, and the consulate makes the decision.

- **Use the size on your checklist.** Almost every Schengen country asks for 35 × 45 mm. The checklist for your consulate and visa type is the final word, including how many photos to bring.
- **Bring a spare.** If a print is damaged or doesn't match the rules, a second copy from the same file saves another trip.
- **Don't trim a U.S. 2 × 2 photo.** The face usually ends up too small for the 32 mm minimum. Make a new 35 × 45 mm photo instead.
- **Don't staple or glue it** unless your checklist tells you to. Some forms have a box for the photo; others ask you to bring it loose.
- **Your biometrics are separate.** First-time applicants usually give fingerprints at the center. That doesn't replace the printed photo if your checklist asks for one.

## Where to print a Schengen visa photo in the U.S.

Most U.S. photo counters default to the 2 × 2 inch U.S. size, so make the photo here and print it yourself:

1. Make and check your photo with the tool on this page.
2. Use the [print passport photos page](/tool/print-passport-photos) to put several 35 × 45 mm copies on one 4 × 6 inch sheet.
3. Order a standard 4 × 6 print at a pharmacy kiosk or online photo service, or print at home on photo paper.
4. Cut out the photos and measure one with a ruler: 35 mm wide and 45 mm tall.

Before your appointment, you can run the photo through the [photo checker](/checker/test-passport-photo) as a second look. If you would rather have a studio or pharmacy take the photo, call ahead and ask for a 35 × 45 mm Schengen visa photo by name; the [guide](/guide/schengen-visa-photo-size) lists the options.

## What the tool can't judge

The tool checks the size, head height, eye line and background. It can't judge your expression, lighting or clothing, and it isn't an official check: the consulate decides whether your photo is accepted. Common reasons for rejection, such as shadows, smiling, glare from glasses and old photos, are covered in the guide's checklist.
