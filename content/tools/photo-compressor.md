## How to compress a photo

1. **Choose your photo.** Select or drag in a JPEG, PNG, or WebP image. It opens in your browser and is never uploaded.
2. **Pick a target size.** Use a preset — 240 KB for U.S. visa applications, 500 KB, or 1 MB — or type any size from 50 to 10,000 KB.
3. **Compress and download.** The tool shows the new file size, dimensions, and quality, so you can confirm it meets the form's limit before downloading.

## How it works

The tool converts your image to JPEG and searches for the highest quality setting that keeps the file at or under your target. If even a low quality setting is too large, it gradually reduces the image dimensions and tries again. It stops at 600 pixels on the shorter side, because many official upload forms — including U.S. visa applications — require at least 600 × 600 pixels.

Transparent areas in PNG images are filled with white, since JPEG doesn't support transparency and ID photos need a light background.

## Common upload limits

| Use | Typical requirement |
| --- | --- |
| U.S. visa (DS-160) photo | Square JPEG, 600 × 600 to 1200 × 1200 px, 240 KB or less |
| Online forms with a 500 KB limit | Choose the 500 KB preset |
| Email attachments | 1 MB keeps photos easy to send |

Requirements vary by form and can change, so check the instructions on the form you're completing. For U.S. visa photos, the Department of State's [digital image requirements](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/photos/digital-image-requirements.html) are the authority.

## Tips for the best result

- **Crop first, then compress.** A square passport-style crop has fewer pixels than a full phone photo, so it compresses with less quality loss. Use the [passport photo maker](/tool/passport-photo-maker), then compress its 600 × 600 output.
- **Start from the original.** Each round of JPEG compression loses detail. Compress the original file once rather than compressing a copy that's already been compressed.
- **Check the result.** Look at the preview at full size. If faces look blocky, choose a larger target if the form allows it.

## What this tool doesn't change

The compressor only changes file size and, when necessary, pixel dimensions. It doesn't crop, straighten, or edit your photo, and it can't make a non-compliant photo meet official requirements. For the full list of rules, read our [passport photo requirements guide](/guide/passport-photo-requirements).
