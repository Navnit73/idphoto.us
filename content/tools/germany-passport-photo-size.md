German passport photos are 35 × 45 mm, in portrait format, and in Germany they now have to be digital. This page explains the German passport photo size and requirements, shows how German visa photos differ, and lets you prepare a 35 × 45 mm biometric photo online.

**Quick answer:** the photo is 35 mm wide and 45 mm high, with the face 32–36 mm tall from chin to top of head (70–80% of the photo), shown from the front with a neutral expression, against a plain light background. For a German passport or ID card application **in Germany**, you can't hand in a printed or self-made photo. At German missions **abroad** and for visas, printed photos are still used.

## Germany Passport Photo Requirements and Guidelines

### What are the Germany passport photo requirements?

The photo must be a recent, sharp, front-facing portrait that shows your face from chin to top of head and both sides of the face. The main rules published by the Federal Foreign Office and its missions are:

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, portrait.
- **Face height:** 32–36 mm, which is 70–80% of the photo.
- **Face position:** centered, with the head upright and not tilted or turned.
- **Expression:** neutral, mouth closed, eyes open and clearly visible.
- **Image quality:** sharp, with good contrast and natural skin tones.

The BMI publishes a [Fotomustertafel](http://www.bmi.bund.de/SharedDocs/downloads/DE/veroeffentlichungen/themen/moderne-verwaltung/ausweise/fotomustertafel.html) with example photos for adults and children.

### German passport picture rules for facial expression and head positioning

German missions use a checklist, the [Passschablone](https://kairo.diplo.de/blob/1505158/2198ba562da42ec69f29352054951703/passschablone-data.pdf), with these questions: is the head straight (not tilted, turned or lowered), is the photo a front view, is the expression neutral with closed lips, is the nose roughly on the center line, and are both eyes open, visible and at the same height inside the marked eye area. Smiling, looking away and a tilted head are the usual reasons for failing these checks.

### Germany passport photo background, lighting and clothing requirements

- **Background:** plain, light and single-colour, with no pattern, and ideally neutral grey. According to the German representation in Ramallah, a medium grey suits light hair and a light grey suits dark hair, so your face and hair stand out.
- **Lighting:** even light on the face, with no reflections, shadows or red eyes. The photo may show only you, with no other people or objects.
- **Glasses:** allowed only if the eyes are clearly visible, with no reflections, tinted lenses or sunglasses, and frames that don't cover the eyes.
- **Head coverings:** generally not allowed. A religious head covering can be accepted if your face stays visible from the chin to the forehead.
- **Clothing:** the sources don't set clothing rules for adults, but clothing close to the background colour makes your outline harder to read.

### What makes a German passport photo acceptable?

A photo is acceptable when it meets the size, face-height, head-position, lighting and background rules above, is unaltered, and reaches the office in the way required for where you apply. Digitally altered photos are not accepted. The German Embassy in Cairo, for example, lists "not digitally changed" on its visa checklist.

## Germany Passport Photo Size and Dimensions

### What is the standard Germany passport photo size in mm?

**35 × 45 mm** (3.5 × 4.5 cm, or about 1.38 × 1.77 inches), portrait. The BMI states that printed photos accepted abroad are 45 mm high by 35 mm wide, in portrait format and without a border.

### Germany passport photo dimensions in pixels

Official German sources give the size in millimeters, not as a pixel count, so the figures below are conversions at common resolutions.

| Resolution | Photo (35 × 45 mm)  | Face height (32–36 mm) |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 300 DPI    | about 413 × 531 px  | about 378–425 px       |
| 600 DPI    | about 827 × 1063 px | about 756–850 px       |

One inch is 25.4 mm, so pixels = millimeters ÷ 25.4 × DPI. The German sample sheet recommends printing at high quality, so a 600 DPI file is a good choice for prints.

### German biometric photo size and face positioning

The face should fill 70–80% of the photo's height, which is 31.5–36 mm of the 45 mm height. A face height below 32 mm is too small. Keep the face centered, with the nose near the vertical center line and the eyes level inside the area marked on the Passschablone.

### Germany passport photo measurements and cropping guidelines

Crop to a 7:9 portrait ratio (35:45), keep your whole head in the frame including your hairstyle if you can without making the face smaller, and don't add a border. Children up to the age of 10 may have a smaller face: the face can take up 50–80% of the photo, or 22–36 mm, according to the German Embassy in Santo Domingo. For babies and children under six, more exceptions are possible.

## German Biometric Passport Photo Requirements

### What is a biometric passport photo in Germany?

A biometric passport photo in Germany is a portrait that meets the standards for German identity documents, so that the face can be read by software and compared at border control. It follows the same 35 × 45 mm format and face-height rules described on this page, and the BMI Fotomustertafel shows examples of accepted and rejected photos.

### German biometric photo requirements for face positioning

Face the camera directly, with your head upright and centered, your face fully visible from chin to top of head, and both eyes open and visible. The Passschablone also checks that the nose is roughly on the center line and the eyes are level.

### How to take a biometric picture with a neutral facial expression

Relax your face, close your mouth, look straight into the lens and don't smile. A neutral expression with closed lips is a requirement on the Passschablone checklist. Take several pictures and choose the one where your eyes are fully open.

### How to check whether your German biometric photo meets official standards

Hold your photo against the Passschablone or compare it with the Fotomustertafel: check the size, the 32–36 mm face height, head position, expression, sharpness and background. The maker's result page shows your measured head height and position, so you can check this before you print. The final decision rests with the authority or mission that receives your application.

## German Visa Photo Requirements and Specifications

### What are the German visa photo requirements?

German visa photos follow the German biometric standards. The German Embassy in London asks for a recent photo (no older than three months), a front view, a face taking up 70–80% of the photo (32–36 mm), a neutral expression with both eyes open, and a white or off-white background. Glasses are allowed if the frames don't cover the eyes, and hats or head coverings are not, except religious ones that leave the face fully visible. Photos copied or scanned from a driver's license or another official document are not accepted.

### Germany visa photo size and dimensions

Visa photos are printed on photo-quality paper at **35 × 45 mm**. The German Embassy in London states this requirement for all photos.

### German visa photo specifications for background and facial positioning

Requirements vary by mission. London asks for a white or off-white background, and Cairo asks for white or light grey and no digital changes, while the general guidance for German passports describes a plain light background, ideally neutral grey. The face must be centered and show a neutral expression. Photo age also varies: three months in London, six months in Cairo. Use the checklist of the mission or visa center where you apply.

## How to Take a Biometric Photo for a German Passport at Home

A photo taken at home suits printed photos for visas and for applications at German missions abroad. For a passport or ID card application in Germany, it can only serve as practice, because the photo must be digital and come from the authority or a certified provider.

### How to take a German biometric photo using a smartphone

Place the phone at eye level on a stand or have someone hold it, set it to the rear camera, turn off filters and beauty modes, and keep your head straight. Take several pictures and keep the sharpest.

### Best lighting and background for German passport pictures

Face a window or use soft, even light from the front, so the light falls on both sides of your face. Stand in front of a plain, light, pattern-free wall or sheet, and step away from it so your shadow doesn't land on it. Avoid reflections, shadows on the face and red eyes.

### How to position your head and shoulders correctly

Keep your head upright and centered, with your face turned straight to the camera and your shoulders level. Your whole head, including your hairstyle if possible, should be in the frame without making your face smaller than 32 mm.

### Common mistakes to avoid when taking a German passport photo

- A smile, an open mouth or closed eyes.
- A tilted, turned or lowered head.
- Shadows on the face or on the wall, or reflections on glasses.
- A face that is too small (under 32 mm) because the camera was too far away.
- A patterned, dark or uneven background.
- Filters, retouching or other digital changes.
- A photo that is too old for your mission's rule.

## How to Get and Submit a German Passport Photo

### How to get a biometric passport photo in Germany

In Germany, the BMI says you have two ways: have the photo taken electronically at the passport or ID authority, if it offers this, or have a photographer or other provider create it and send it electronically through a secure channel. Ask your local office whether it has a photo station.

### Can you submit a German passport photo digitally?

In Germany, you must: since 1 May 2025 printed passport photos are no longer accepted for passport and ID card applications, and studio photos are accepted only when they reach the authority through secure electronic channels. You can't upload a file of your own. At German missions abroad, live capture is not yet available and photographers abroad can't be certified for electronic transmission, so printed photos are still used. For visas, follow your mission's instructions.

### Where to get a German passport photo taken

In Germany, use a photo station at the authority or a certified photo provider. Abroad, some German missions have a photo machine on site, for example the Embassy in Brussels and the Embassy in London, or you can use a photo studio that follows the German format.

### How to prepare a German passport photo for official submission

1. Check whether you apply in Germany, at a German mission abroad, or for a visa.
2. Read the instructions of the office or mission, including photo age and background colour.
3. Check your photo against the [Fotomustertafel](http://www.bmi.bund.de/SharedDocs/downloads/DE/veroeffentlichungen/themen/moderne-verwaltung/ausweise/fotomustertafel.html) and the Passschablone.
4. For a print, use photo-quality paper at actual size with no scaling, with no border, and measure the result: 35 × 45 mm. A 4 × 6 inch sheet holds six photos of this size (two across, three down). See our guide to [printing passport photos](/tool/print-passport-photos).
5. Bring the original documents and any other items your office or mission asks for.
