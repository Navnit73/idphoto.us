## ID Photo Size, Dimensions and Requirements

There is no single ID photo size. The issuer of each document sets its own, so check the rules for your document first. For U.S. passport-style photos, the Department of State sets these numbers.

| Item              | U.S. passport-style photo                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Size              | 2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm), square                    |
| Pixels at 300 DPI | 600 × 600 px                                     |
| Head height       | 1 to 1⅜ in (25–35 mm), chin to top of head       |
| Background        | White or off-white, no shadows, texture or lines |
| Expression        | Neutral, both eyes open, mouth closed            |
| Photo age         | Taken within the last 6 months                   |

### What is the standard ID photo size in the USA?

For a U.S. passport, the standard is **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)**. Other U.S. IDs don't share one national photo size, so a driver's license, a work badge and a school card can each follow different rules.

### How to make a 2x2 ID photo online

A 2x2 photo is 2 inches wide and 2 inches tall. At 300 dots per inch (DPI), that is 2 × 300 = **600 pixels** on each side. A 600 × 600 pixel file printed at 300 DPI comes out at exactly 2 × 2 inches. For online visa forms, the digital file must be square, from 600 × 600 to 1200 × 1200 pixels, in JPEG format and sRGB color, and no larger than 240 kB. Use the [2x2 photo maker](/tool/2x2-photo-maker) for the full size guide.

### How to create a 1x1 ID picture online

A 1x1 picture is a square photo 1 inch (25.4 mm) wide and tall, which is 300 × 300 pixels at 300 DPI. This page doesn't cite an official U.S. standard for 1x1 photos, so don't assume the 2 × 2 inch head-height range scales down. Read the instructions from the organization that asked for the photo and follow its size and head-height rules.

### What are the background, face positioning and lighting requirements for ID photos?

- **Background:** plain white or off-white, without shadows, texture or lines.
- **Face position:** look straight at the camera without tilting your head, with your full face in view.
- **Lighting:** use even light on your face. Overhead lights or lights far to one side can cast shadows, and light that is too bright or too dim spoils the exposure.
- **Glasses:** U.S. passport photos don't allow them, unless you can't remove them for a medical reason and include a signed note from your doctor.
- **Head coverings:** allowed only for religious or medical reasons with a signed statement, and they can't cast shadows or hide your face.

## How to Edit and Customize an ID Photo Online

### How to change the background of an ID photo

For a U.S. passport photo, the background must be white or off-white without shadows, texture or lines, and the Department of State doesn't allow changing a photo with software, apps, filters or AI. The most reliable fix is a retake against a plain wall.

### How to resize and crop an ID picture online

Cropping and resizing are fine for U.S. passport photos. The editor crops your picture to a square and sizes your head within the allowed range. Head size is the most common problem in home photos: stand too close and your head is too big, stand too far back and it's too small.

### How to create a formal ID picture using an online editor

Wear plain clothing that contrasts with the background. For a U.S. passport photo, don't wear a uniform, clothing that looks like a uniform, or camouflage, and don't wear headphones or wireless hands-free devices. Keep a neutral expression, with both eyes open and your mouth closed.

### How to improve ID photo lighting and remove unwanted shadows

Face a window with indirect daylight and stand a short distance in front of the wall, so your shadow doesn't land on it. Avoid ceiling lights directly above you. Editing can't remove shadows reliably, so retake the photo if you see them.

## Create Different Types of ID Photos Online

### How to create a US ID photo online

"US ID photo" covers several documents, and they don't follow the same rules.

USCIS changed its photo policy on December 12, 2025. For requests filed on or after that date, it no longer accepts self-submitted photos, and photos are taken by USCIS or an authorized entity. Printed form instructions may not reflect this yet, so follow the current instructions for your form on uscis.gov, not an old printout.

### How to make a passport ID photo online

You can take or buy your own photo for a U.S. passport. It must be printed on matte or glossy photo-quality paper, and photocopies and scans aren't accepted. For digital visa uploads, use a square JPEG in sRGB color, from 600 × 600 to 1200 × 1200 pixels and no larger than 240 kB. The embassy or consulate makes the final decision on acceptance.

### How to create a driver's license photo online

Many driver's license photos are taken by the DMV at the office, and rules vary by state. New York, for example, tells people without a photo license or ID that they must visit a DMV office to have their photo taken at no charge. Check your state's DMV website before you prepare a photo yourself.

### How to make an ID card photo for work, school or other applications

Employers and schools set their own photo rules, such as size, background color and file format. Ask the issuer for its specification before you upload anything, then check the maker's result against it.

## How to Print an ID Photo Online

The editor downloads a single 600 × 600 pixel photo. Print it on matte or glossy photo-quality paper.

### How to print an ID photo at home

Print at **actual size** with no scaling, because "fit to page" changes the dimensions. Measure one printed photo with a ruler before you cut: it must be exactly 2 inches on each side. Photocopies and scans of a photo aren't accepted for U.S. passports.

### How to arrange multiple ID photos on a 4x6 photo sheet

Place copies on a 4 × 6 inch canvas in any photo editor, then order a standard 4 × 6 print at a pharmacy or print shop, or print it at home. A 4 × 6 inch print holds **six** 2x2 photos, two across and three down, because 4 ÷ 2 = 2 and 6 ÷ 2 = 3. With six photos there's no space between them. Most applications need one or two photos, so a sheet with two photos and wide margins is easier to cut. Our guide to [printing passport photos](/tool/print-passport-photos) gives exact layouts for two, four and six photos.

### How to print a 2x2 ID photo in the correct dimensions

At 300 DPI, a 600 × 600 pixel file prints at exactly 2 × 2 inches. A larger square file, such as 1200 × 1200, prints at the same size if your printer scales it to 2 inches. Turn off "fit to page" and any automatic scaling.

### How to save an ID photo for professional printing

Keep the original 600 × 600 pixel file and send it to the print shop without resizing, so the shop doesn't scale it. Tell them to print at 2 × 2 inches, and measure one print before you leave.

## Common ID Photo Problems and How to Fix Them

### Why does my ID photo look blurry or stretched?

Blurry photos usually come from a low-resolution or heavily compressed original. Retake the photo in good light and don't enlarge a small image. Stretching comes from a non-square crop or from a print scaled with "fit to page", so use a square file and print at actual size.

### How to fix incorrect ID photo dimensions

Check the size of your file and your print. A U.S. passport-style photo must be a square 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), which is 600 × 600 pixels at 300 DPI, with the head 1 to 1⅜ inches (25–35 mm) tall. If your head is too big, you were too close to the camera. If it's too small, you were too far away. Retake the photo from a better distance.

### How to remove shadows and uneven backgrounds from an ID picture

Shadows on your face or on the wall are the most common reason for rejection, and editing can't fix them reliably. Face a window with indirect daylight, step away from the wall, and avoid ceiling lights directly above you. Then retake the photo.

### How to make an ID photo look professional and natural

Use a neutral expression, plain clothing and even light. Don't retouch the photo or use filters, skin smoothing or AI changes, which U.S. passport photos don't accept. Cropping and resizing are fine.
