If you're a citizen of an EU country living in the U.S., you'll renew your passport through your country's embassy or consulates, and each country sets its own photo rules. **There is no single "EU passport photo size."** Most countries use 35 × 45 mm, but the head-height rule, background colour and whether you bring a photo at all differ from one country to the next. This page compares ten countries in one place, with each rule taken from that government's own pages.

**Quick answer:** the most common size is **35 × 45 mm (1.38 × 1.77 inches)**, about the size of the photo printed in the passport. Greece uses 40 × 60 mm. Sweden and many Belgian posts take the photo themselves, so you don't bring one. A U.S. 2 × 2 inch photo is accepted only by a few consulates.

## Why there's no single EU passport photo size

EU law, [Regulation (EC) No 2252/2004](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02004R2252-20090626), requires every member state's passport to hold a facial image and two fingerprints on a secure chip, and to follow the international standard for machine-readable passports, [ICAO Doc 9303](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/publications/DocSeries/9303_p3_cons_en.pdf). Fingerprints aren't required for children under 12.

The regulation doesn't set the size of the printed photo you submit, its background, or the head height. Each country decides those. That's why a Dutch photo, with its 26–30 mm head, is too small for a German passport, which needs 32–36 mm, even though both are 35 × 45 mm. The German missions in the Netherlands [warn about exactly this](https://niederlande.diplo.de/nl-nl/service/reisepaesse/2766890-2766890).

## Dutch passport photo size

The Dutch government's [photo requirements](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/passport-id-card/photo-requirements) set a **35 × 45 mm** photo with the head **26–30 mm** from chin to crown for applicants aged 11 and over (19–30 mm for children aged 10 and under), on a light grey, light blue or white background, no more than six months old when you apply.

From the U.S., you [apply in person](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/passport-id-card/abroad/apply-united-states) at the embassy in Washington, D.C., the consulates-general in Atlanta, Miami, New York or San Francisco, or a VFS Global office in Boston, Chicago, Houston or Los Angeles. You bring printed photos, and the Netherlands publishes a [list of U.S. photographers](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/countries/united-states/living-and-working/photo-for-a-passport-or-identity-card) familiar with its rules. If you use another photographer, take the Dutch "photo matrix" with you.

The Dutch head height is smaller than in most other countries, so a photo made for a German or French passport is likely to fail, and the other way round.

## French passport photo size

[Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F10619?lang=en) sets the French passport photo at **3.5 × 4.5 cm** with the face **3.2–3.6 cm** from chin to top of skull, excluding hair (70–80% of the photo). The background must be plain and light, such as light blue or light grey. **White isn't allowed**, which surprises many people used to U.S. photos. The photo must be less than 6 months old and taken by an approved professional or an approved photo booth.

French consulates in the U.S. require you to [appear in person](https://us.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/identite/passeport-0) so they can take your fingerprints, and you bring the photo yourself. The Consulate General in Los Angeles has a paid photo booth, which isn't suitable for infants.

**About the e-photo code:** in France, some photo booths give a digital code instead of prints for certain applications. In a [September 2026 reply](https://www.plus.transformation.gouv.fr/experiences/7527977_cout-financier-injustifie-et-gaspillage-impose-pour-les-photos-de-passeport), France Titres said extending the e-photo to passport and ID card applications is still being studied. For a passport from the U.S., bring a printed photo.

## German passport photo size

German passport photos are **35 × 45 mm** with the face **32–36 mm** tall for applicants aged 10 and over. The [German missions in the U.S.](https://www.germany.info/us-en/service/02-PassportsandIDCards/passport-adult/951294) ask for two identical, recent biometric photos, tell you not to cut the photos yourself, and take your fingerprints at an in-person appointment.

Since May 2025, passport applications **in Germany** need a digital photo sent by a certified provider. That rule doesn't apply at missions abroad yet: the German Embassy in Brussels says [live photo capture isn't available](https://bruessel.diplo.de/be-de/service/2639128-2639128) at German missions abroad, so applications there still use printed photos.

For sizes in pixels, the full requirements and German visa photos, see our [Germany passport photo guide](/tool/germany-passport-photo-size).

## Italian, Spanish and Polish passport photos

### Italy

Italian consulates in the U.S. set their own photo rules. The [Consulate General in New York](https://consnewyork.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/passaporti-e-carte-didentita/how-do-i-apply-for-a-passport/) asks for 2 recent, identical, standard passport-size photos on a white background, without glasses, and can take the photos at the consulate for applicants over 12. The [Consulate General in San Francisco](https://conssanfrancisco.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/passaporti-e-carte-didentita/passports-for-adults/) accepts Italian-size or U.S. passport-size photos. Other Italian consulates may differ.

### Spain

The [Spanish Consulate General in New York](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Consulados/nuevayork/es/ServiciosConsulares/Paginas/index.aspx?scca=Pasaportes+y+otros+documentos&scco=Estados+Unidos&scd=215&scs=Pasaportes+-+Requisitos+y+procedimiento+para+obtenerlo) asks for **1 recent colour photo of 32 × 26 mm** on a white background, taken from the front, and says **2 × 2 inch photos are also valid**. You apply in person, and fingerprints are taken from applicants over 12.

### Poland

Poland's [photo rules](https://www.gov.pl/web/gov/zdjecie-do-dowodu-lub-paszportu) set a **35 × 45 mm** colour photo with the face taking up 70–80% of it, a white, evenly lit background, taken no more than 6 months before you apply. The [Polish Embassy in the U.S.](https://www.gov.pl/web/usa-en/passport-for-an-adult) says passport applications must be submitted in person, with a photo attached, and that the consul won't accept an application whose photo doesn't meet the requirements.

## Irish, Greek, Swedish and Belgian passport photos

### Ireland: apply online from the U.S.

Ireland is the exception: Irish passports can be [renewed online from anywhere in the world](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/passports/how-to-apply-for-a-passport/renew-adult-passport/), with a postal fee if you live outside Ireland. The [photo guidelines](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/passports/photo-guidelines/) ask for a digital photo of **at least 715 × 951 pixels**, and a selfie isn't accepted. Paper applications need 4 identical photos, no more than 6 months old, between 35 × 45 mm and 38 × 50 mm, on a plain light grey, white or cream background. Ireland's Photo Code service, which links printed photos to the online form, is only offered in Ireland and the UK.

### Greece: 40 × 60 mm

Greek passport photos are larger than most: **40 × 60 mm**, without a frame, on a light background (preferably grey), printed on photographic paper and taken within the last six months, according to the [Hellenic Police passport authority](https://passport.gov.gr/en/diadikasia-ekdosis/documents/specificationphoto.html). From abroad, you provide the photo as a print at the consular office, where it is digitized; Greece's myPhoto service only works inside Greece. Fingerprints are taken except from children under 12. The government's [procedures register](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issuance_of_a_Greek_passport) says the photo must be taken within the last month, which is stricter than the six months above, so a very recent photo is safest.

### Sweden: don't bring photos

The [Embassy of Sweden in Washington](https://swedenabroad.se/en/about-abroad-for-swedish-citizens/usa/service-for-swedish-citizens/passports/passports-for-adults) says: "Do not bring passport photos." All applicants are photographed digitally at the time of application, at the embassy, the consulates-general in New York and San Francisco, or some honorary consulates.

### Belgium: often taken on site

Belgium's foreign ministry says [many embassies and consulates take the photo themselves](https://diplomatie.belgium.be/en/belgians-abroad/belgian-passport/ordinary-passport), so you don't need to bring one, and fingerprints are taken from age 12. In the U.S., the embassy's [Flying Consul](https://unitedstates.diplomatie.belgium.be/en/consular-services/flying-consul-missions) travels with a mobile kit to record your photo, fingerprints and signature. Ask your post whether to bring a photo.

## Can you use a U.S. 2x2 photo for an EU passport?

Usually not. A U.S. passport photo is 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) and square, with a different head size. The consulates that do accept U.S. sizes say so on their pages:

- **Accepted:** Spain (New York consulate), Italy (New York and San Francisco consulates).
- **Not accepted:** Greece (40 × 60 mm), Germany and the Netherlands (35 × 45 mm with country-specific head heights), France (35 × 45 mm, and not on a white background).
- **Not needed:** Sweden, and many Belgian posts, which take the photo themselves.

If your consulate accepts 2 × 2, the [2x2 photo maker](/tool/2x2-photo-maker) makes one.

## Passport photo vs Schengen visa photo

If you're not an EU citizen and you need a visa to visit Europe, you need a Schengen visa photo, not a passport photo. It's also 35 × 45 mm but follows visa rules; see the [Schengen visa photo guide](/guide/schengen-visa-photo-size).
