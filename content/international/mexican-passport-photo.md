If you're a Mexican national in the U.S. searching for a Mexican passport picture, here is the short answer: **you don't need to bring one.** The consulate takes your photo at the appointment, with its own equipment, and that's the photo printed in your passport. This page explains how that photo is taken and what to wear, then covers the Mexican documents and procedures that **do** still ask for a printed photo, and the size they use.

**Quick answer:** Mexican passport: no photo needed, it's taken on site. When an SRE form asks for a "tamaño pasaporte" photo: 3.5 × 4.5 cm (about 1.38 × 1.77 in), in colour, from the front, white background, no glasses, head uncovered.

## Do you need a photo for a Mexican passport?

No. Mexico's [passport regulation](https://embamex.sre.gob.mx/espana/images/reglamentopasaportes.pdf) (Reglamento de Pasaportes, articles 14 and 18) requires adults and minors to **allow themselves to be photographed with the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores' (SRE) equipment**. The consular section in Washington, D.C. says it directly on its [passport requirements sheet](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/washington/images/2020/documentacion/Requisitos_para_PASAPORTE_2020.pdf): "Para realizar el trámite de Pasaporte NO es necesario traer fotografías" (you don't need to bring photos for a passport).

This is the same at consulates in the U.S. and at SRE offices in Mexico. It also applies to children: the parent or guardian authorizes the photo, and the child is photographed at the appointment.

So if a photo shop offers you "Mexican passport photos", you don't need them for the passport itself. Save your money, or check whether you need one of the [documents that still use printed photos](#which-mexican-documents-still-need-a-printed-photo).

### Why the photo is taken at the consulate

Since 2021, Mexico issues an electronic passport. According to the [SRE](https://www.gob.mx/sre/prensa/sre-efectua-ceremonia-sobre-generacion-de-llaves-electronicas-para-el-nuevo-pasaporte-mexicano), each passport has a chip that stores your photo, and the passport follows International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) standards. The first electronic passports were issued in October 2021 in Mexico City and at the [Los Angeles consulate](https://www.gob.mx/sre/prensa/canciller-marcelo-ebrard-entrega-primer-pasaporte-electronico-mexicano?idiom=es). Capturing the photo, fingerprints and signature at the appointment keeps this data consistent and hard to tamper with. The SRE's [passport procedure page](https://www.gob.mx/sre/acciones-y-programas/tramite-de-pasaporte-8014) lists photo, fingerprints, iris recognition and signature among the data taken.

## How the Mexican passport photo is taken, and what to wear

The regulation sets the rules for the on-site photo: it is taken **from the front, in colour, with a white background, with your head uncovered, and without clothing or objects that partly or fully cover your face, including glasses**.

What that means for your appointment:

- **Glasses:** you'll take them off. They aren't allowed in the photo.
- **Hats, caps and head coverings:** remove them; the head must be uncovered.
- **Hair:** keep it away from your face so nothing covers it.
- **Clothing:** SRE doesn't publish a dress code. Because the background is white, a darker or coloured top makes your outline clearer. That's our suggestion, not an official rule.
- **Expression:** face the camera directly. Consulates photograph you on the spot, so you can't retake it at home; arrive rested and ready.

Some consulates outside the U.S. publish extra tips such as moderate makeup or visible ears. These aren't national rules, so follow what your consulate tells you.

## How to get a Mexican passport in the U.S.

1. **Book an appointment** through MiConsulado: WhatsApp or phone at **1 (424) 309-0009**, or online at citas.sre.gob.mx. These are the channels listed by the [Mexican Consulate in San Francisco](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/sanfrancisco/index.php/documentos-de-identidad/pasaporte). Older guides list a MEXITEL number; use MiConsulado instead.
2. **Gather your documents:** proof of Mexican nationality and an official photo ID, as listed by your consulate. For a minor, both parents or guardians usually need to be there or give consent.
3. **Go to the appointment.** The consulate takes your photo, fingerprints and signature. You don't need printed photos.

For a child under 7 abroad without a photo ID, the regulation lets parents present a **medical certificate with the child's photo** and the stamp of a medical institution recognized where the family lives. That is an ID document, not a passport photo. Ask your consulate how it wants it prepared.

### Which Mexican documents still need a printed photo?

The SRE's nationality forms, the [certificate of Mexican nationality (DNN-1)](https://sre.gob.mx/images/stories/docnatnacio/dnn1.pdf), the [declaration of Mexican nationality (DNN-2)](https://sre.gob.mx/images/stories/docnatnacio/dnn2.pdf) and the [naturalization letter (DNN-3)](https://sre.gob.mx/images/stories/docnatnacio/dnn3.pdf), all use the same wording: **two identical, recent colour photos, passport size (4.5 × 3.5 cm), white background, facing front, no glasses, head uncovered.**

Be aware that these forms are older than recent changes. In June 2026, news reports said the SRE had simplified nationality and naturalization procedures and removed the photo requirement from several of them. Before you print photos for one of these procedures, ask the consulate or SRE office handling it whether it still wants them.

### Mexican visa photo for non-Mexicans

If you're not Mexican and you need a Mexican visa from a U.S. consulate, the photo rules depend on the consulate. The consular section in Washington, D.C.'s [2026 checklist](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/washington/images/2026/visas/esp/Sin_Actividades_Lucrativas_Turismo_Negocios_Trnsito.pdf) for visitor visas asks for one passport-size colour photo, from the front, without glasses, on a white background. Other consulates list their own sizes, and Mexico's 2025 visa guidelines move toward capturing biometric data instead of collecting photos. Use the checklist of the consulate where you apply.

## Mexico passport picture size for printed photos

When a Mexican form asks for a "fotografía tamaño pasaporte", the SRE forms define it as **3.5 × 4.5 cm**.

| Measurement                  | Value                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Size (width × height)        | 3.5 × 4.5 cm (35 × 45 mm)     |
| In inches                    | about 1.38 × 1.77 in          |
| At 300 DPI                   | about 413 × 531 pixels        |
| Background                   | White                         |
| Colour                       | Colour                        |
| Pose                         | From the front, head uncovered |
| Glasses                      | Not allowed                   |

This isn't the same as a U.S. 2 × 2 inch (5.1 × 5.1 cm) photo, which is larger and square. Don't bring a 2 × 2 print unless your consulate's checklist allows it.
